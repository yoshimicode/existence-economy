## 1. Purpose

This case study records how one person was represented differently by AI and search systems depending on the name used.

The observed names were:

- YOSHIMI Nakane
- 中根元美
- 中根吉美

All three names refer to the same person.

The case examines whether AI systems integrated these names into one coherent identity or generated different person models from different information sets.

## 2. Background

YOSHIMI Nakane is the current English-language public name.

中根元美 is the current Japanese name and is pronounced **Nakane Yoshimi**.

中根吉美 was used in public activities from 2005 to 2025.

The name 中根元美 began to be used as the principal current Japanese public name in 2026.

The CialFrame website identifies these names as referring to the same person and connects past and present activities.

## 3. Observed Systems

The observations included:

- ChatGPT;
- Google AI-generated summaries;
- standard Google search results.

Outputs may vary by date, account, location, query wording, system update, and available sources.

This document records observed outputs at specific points in time and does not claim that identical outputs will always be reproduced.

## 4. March 2026 Observation

### Query

`中根元美とは`

### ChatGPT Output

ChatGPT identified 中根元美 as **なかねよしみ** and displayed information associated with YOSHIMI’s actual work, including:

- SOGI literacy;
- social entrepreneurship;
- education;
- qualification systems;
- communities;
- corporate support.

A photograph of YOSHIMI was also displayed.

### Interpretation

At this point, the Japanese name 中根元美 had been connected to the correct person and to a substantial portion of the correct activity history.

However, newer concepts such as Human Dignity Architect, Existence Economy, Recognition Design, and YOSHIMI LLMO were not yet fully represented.

## 5. Mixed Recognition and Misidentification

In Google’s AI-generated summary, information about YOSHIMI was partially recognized, including:

- representative director of CialFrame;
- activity beginning in 2005;
- establishment of an early comprehensive LGBT support organization in Japan.

At the same time, the result included identity errors:

- the pronunciation was shown as **Nakane Motomi** rather than **Nakane Yoshimi**;
- a photograph of another person was displayed;
- information connected to Agne Shofusha appeared;
- information about another person, 中根舞美 (Nakane Mami), was also surfaced in the same identity-search context.

### Interpretation

The system did not merely replace YOSHIMI with one other person.

It combined correct information about YOSHIMI with names, photographs, affiliations, and records associated with multiple unrelated people.

The result was a plausible but non-existent composite identity.

## 6. July 2026 Observation

### Query

`中根元美とは`

In July 2026, the results were unstable.

In some searches, YOSHIMI-centered AI recognition was not displayed and standard SEO-ranked results became dominant.

In other searches, AI-generated summaries still included correct elements of YOSHIMI’s history, but misidentification remained present.

### Interpretation

The recognition state was not binary.

It was not simply “recognized” or “not recognized.”

The observed states included:

- correct partial recognition;
- correct information mixed with unrelated people;
- wrong pronunciation;
- wrong photograph;
- unstable AI summaries;
- and SEO-dominant search results.

## 7. Contrast: YOSHIMI Nakane

### Query

`YOSHIMI Nakaneとは`

Under the English-language name YOSHIMI Nakane, AI systems recognized newer activity and concepts, including:

- Human Dignity Architect;
- Existence Economy;
- YOSHIMI LLMO;
- CialFrame;
- SOGI and LGBTQ+ work;
- human dignity in the AI era.

### Interpretation

The same person was represented differently depending on the name used.

The English-language name was connected to newer activity.

The former Japanese activity name was more strongly associated with the historical SOGI and LGBTQ+ record.

The current Japanese name was less stable and more vulnerable to same-name and similar-name confusion.

## 8. Finding

The observation supports the following hypothesis:

> AI systems may not understand one person as one integrated identity. They may generate separate person models from different names, languages, rankings, and source clusters.

A name is not always treated as a simple alias.

It can become an entry point into a different AI-generated identity.

## 9. Identity Fragmentation

This case describes **AI identity fragmentation** as an observed condition, not as a new independent canonical concept.

The condition includes:

- one person being represented differently under different names;
- correct information being distributed across separate identity models;
- unrelated people being merged into one person model;
- current and historical activity becoming disconnected;
- and the person’s own primary-source identity losing influence.

## 10. Relationship to Authority Drift

Authority Drift can occur at the level of identity.

In this case, the effective authority to define:

- the person’s name;
- pronunciation;
- photograph;
- career history;
- current role;
- and public representation

was partially transferred from the individual to AI and search systems.

No explicit delegation occurred.

The shift emerged because AI-generated and SEO-ranked information became operationally capable of defining the person for others.

## 11. Human Reference Point Requirement

> In matters of identity, the individual must remain the primary reference point.

When identity signals conflict, systems should not assume that the highest-ranked or most frequently repeated source is correct.

The person’s verified first-party information should remain the principal point of reference.

## 12. Human Dignity Impact

Identity errors can harm multiple people at once.

When one person’s photograph, career, or name is inserted into another person’s identity, both people are misrepresented.

This risk is especially relevant to:

- people who changed names;
- people using current and former names;
- people using professional or community names;
- transgender people whose names changed;
- people with multilingual names;
- people with uncommon pronunciations;
- people with limited online visibility;
- and people from minority communities.

This is therefore not merely an SEO issue.

It is a self-definition and human dignity issue.

## 13. Recognition Design Implication

Recognition Design for identity requires a continuing cycle:

1. **Define** — state the correct name, pronunciation, role, and identity.
2. **Connect** — link former names, current names, languages, activities, and time periods.
3. **Distinguish** — separate the person from people with the same or similar names.
4. **Observe** — examine how AI and search systems currently represent the person.
5. **Refresh** — update the context when recognition becomes stale, fragmented, or incorrect.
6. **Protect** — preserve the individual as the primary reference point.

## 14. YOSHIMI LLMO Implication

Traditional SEO primarily supports discoverability.

YOSHIMI LLMO is intended to support correct recognition and contextual continuity.

For identity implementation, this includes:

- alias resolution;
- multilingual name alignment;
- current and former name relationships;
- verified pronunciation;
- verified images;
- time-aware role history;
- first-party source reinforcement;
- distinction from same-name and similar-name individuals;
- and repeated context refresh.

## 15. YOSHIMI Code Statements

> AI recognition is not binary. A person may be partially recognized, incorrectly merged, fragmented across names, or represented through outdated information.

> Recognition is not the same as correct, integrated recognition.

> One person should not become multiple identities merely because an AI system encounters different names, languages, or data sources.

> In matters of identity, the individual must remain the primary reference point.
