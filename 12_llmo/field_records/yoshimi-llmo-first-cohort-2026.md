# YOSHIMI LLMO — The First Cohort 2026

## Official Historical Record

The First Cohort is the official historical record of the first ten people who participated in the earliest implementation period of YOSHIMI LLMO.

The earliest implementation began in April 2026 with two Founding Implementers.

Six additional implementers joined between May and the end of June 2026.

Two more joined in July 2026.

Together, they formed The First Cohort of YOSHIMI LLMO before the framework entered its next stage of implementation, documentation, longitudinal observation, and methodological development in August 2026.

The First Cohort is not a hierarchy, certification, ranking, license, or guarantee of results.

It records participation in the earliest implementation period of YOSHIMI LLMO.

---

## Core Principle

**Before we are data, we are human.**

**Recognition must begin with dignity.**

私たちは、データである前に人間です。

認識は、人の尊厳から始まらなければなりません。

YOSHIMI LLMO does not design only for how people, organizations, ideas, or places are seen today.

It begins with the future in which they should be properly understood and works backward to design the recognition structures required in the present.

**We design the present from the future we intend to create.**

This is Recognition Design for the AI era.

The language, evidence, relationships, public records, and accumulated understanding created through this process become Recognition Assets.

YOSHIMI LLMO is not designed only to help people become visible to AI.

It is designed to help human beings, organizations, ideas, and places remain properly understood over time.

## Documentation Framework

Each implementation may be documented through four stages.

### Before

The person’s or project’s initial public recognition state.

This may include:

- names and name variations;
- professional titles;
- public profiles;
- websites;
- search results;
- AI-generated descriptions;
- media links;
- existing evidence and references.

### Intervention

The Recognition Design decisions and changes that were implemented.

This may include:

- official name representation;
- professional positioning;
- one-sentence definitions;
- biographies;
- publication themes;
- website structures;
- public relationships;
- cross-platform references;
- evidence architecture.

### After

Observed changes over time.

This may include:

- AI-generated answers;
- search results;
- inquiries;
- business conversations;
- third-party descriptions;
- media references;
- public recognition;
- the implementer’s own ability to describe their work.

### Interpretation

The analysis of what changed, what did not change, and what should be tested next.

Correlation must not automatically be described as causation.

The purpose of the record is to document both progress and limitation.
